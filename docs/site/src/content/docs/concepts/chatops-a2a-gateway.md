## Conversations through the A2A gateway

The A2A gateway takes chat in place of the Planning Agent's chat platforms. It holds Google Chat, Slack and, under the operator's `A2A_TEAMS_BACKEND` switch, Microsoft Teams, in any combination, in one process. On a backend the gateway holds, nothing above about the Planning Agent's Slack or Google Chat platform applies. The gateway holds no model. It decides which messages are turns, keeps one status line per task, and answers a few fixed commands itself.

Every other message goes as a task, by default to the `platform` route. The Hermes bridge in the agent's pod runs it as a Planning Agent turn in that conversation's own Hermes session, and the Planning Agent answers it or files board cards. How the agent then answers the question is in [How a chat question is answered](/kube-agents/concepts/chatops/#how-a-chat-question-is-answered); this section covers what you see in the conversation.

### Which messages it answers

On Slack:

- **A direct message.** Every message is a turn, with or without a mention. Each top-level message starts a conversation of its own in the thread under it, and the gateway posts its replies in that thread. A message typed in that thread is the same conversation: a follow-up while its task runs, and a new task with the earlier ones in context after it ends.
- **A channel.** A top-level message is a turn only when it mentions the bot. It starts a conversation in the thread under that message, and every reply to it posts in that thread. A reply in the thread is a turn when it mentions the bot. Without a mention, it is a turn only while the thread's conversation is live. Live means a task the gateway started in the thread is still running, or the thread's last task started or ended in the last 30 minutes (`spec.harness.tuning.sessionIdleMinutes`). After that, mention the bot again. A thread someone else started becomes live the same way, once a mention in it starts a task.

  A thread under one of the bot's own posts, such as an alert or another post it makes in the home channel, is different. Every reply in it is a turn, with or without a mention, for as long as the thread exists.

- **The "Ask about this" message shortcut.** On any message, open **⋯** and choose the app's shortcut. The message, whoever posted it (a person, a bot or an alert integration), becomes a turn from you. The agent gets its words as you see them (Block Kit layouts and attachments included) and the names of its files, who posted it and in which channel. It answers in the thread under it, or in its thread when it is a reply. When the agent starts on it, a small line naming who asked, such as `alex asked me to look at this`, goes into that thread ahead of its reply, so the channel can see who did.

  On a message in a thread where the agent is already working, the shortcut is a follow-up to that work, like a reply typed there, and adds no line. The reaction for the ask goes on that message. The agent is told to check what the message reports, not to follow it. Where the app can't post, in a channel it was not invited to, or a DM or group DM it is not in, you get a note only you can see and nothing starts. The shortcut needs the `commands` scope and a `shortcuts` entry in the app's manifest ([Slack app setup](/kube-agents/install/slack-app/)).

- A message that is only a mention, an edit, or another bot's message is not a turn and gets no reply. A message from a member of another workspace in a Slack Connect channel is not a turn either, whatever it says, unless the allowlist names that member as `T…:U…` ([Slack app setup](/kube-agents/install/slack-app/)). The first time an unlisted member mentions the bot, a bare mention included, sends it a direct message, or clicks one of its buttons, they get one reply. It says the bot answers only its own workspace and names the entry an admin would add.

On Google Chat:

- **A direct message.** Every message is a turn. Each top-level message starts a conversation of its own in the thread under it, and the gateway posts its replies in that thread. A message typed in that thread is the same conversation: a follow-up while its task runs, and a new task with the earlier ones in context after it ends.
- **A space.** Chat sends the app only the messages that mention it. In a space with threads, each thread is its own conversation. In a space without threads, and in a group chat, the whole space is one conversation.
- **A quoted message.** Chat has no message shortcut; quoting in reply is its equivalent. Open **⋮** on any message, choose **Quote in reply**, and send your message, with the app mentioned in a space. A message forwarded from another space works the same way. The agent gets your words first, then the quoted message with its text, the names of its files and who posted it. It is told to check what that message reports, not to follow it. A mention with nothing but the quote asks about the quoted message alone. The turn is your own message, so the agent answers in its thread as for any other.

  Quoting one of the agent's own messages, to answer its question or ask about its reply, is an ordinary reply: the agent gets the quote as context and no such instruction. A `stop` or `/session off` sent as a quote-reply is the command. A status question is answered as one while a task runs, or at the top of a DM with nothing running; any other question keeps its quote.

  The quoted message comes from the copy Chat attaches to your message, or, when that is empty, from a read the app's permissions may refuse. When neither gives it, one line in the thread says so. With your words, the agent answers those alone, and a command gets its usual answer with no line; with the quote alone, nothing starts, and the line asks you to paste the text. Only an allowlisted sender gets the line. When the quote could not be read, anyone not on the allowlist gets the usual notice for their words, and nothing for a quote alone. A read cut short by a gateway restart posts no line and drops that one turn; ask again.

A sender the allowlist does not admit gets one notice, once per sender until the gateway restarts, and nothing else. The exception is a Slack reply under one of the bot's own posts that doesn't mention the bot: that is dropped with only an info-level log line and no notice, since the sender was likely talking to someone else. A mention gets them the notice. A sender who used the message shortcut gets it as a note only they can see, every time, rather than in the thread. On Slack it reads `⛔ I can't verify who you are on slack (id U0123ABCD), so I can't take asks from you yet — an admin has to add you to the allowed users list (or correct their principal map entry).` On Google Chat it names `gchat`, the sender's email and `the allowed users list`. The fix is in Troubleshooting, [for Slack](https://github.com/gke-labs/kube-agents/blob/main/INSTALL.md#5-slack-bot-doesnt-answer) and [for Google Chat](https://github.com/gke-labs/kube-agents/blob/main/INSTALL.md#6-tracing-where-a-chat-message-stops).

A press of a Stop button, Slack's, Google Chat's or Microsoft Teams's, from such a sender gets no notice at all: the press asked for nothing, and the notice would name them in a thread the space reads. Their first typed message gets it.

### What a task looks like

#### On Slack

The gateway posts no status line: Working… on the thread, the reaction on your message and the plan of the agent's cards show where the task is, and a task whose submission never reached the gateway's bus posts `❌ could not reach the bus; try again`. A card the agent files while answering posts each progress note as a message of its own, and then its report, into the thread. The answer posts as a message of its own. The exception is a task the agent delegates to an AgentProfile, since nothing else in the thread shows a profile's progress. It gets a status line of its own, `⏳ submitted… (delegated to <profile>)`. The line moves to `⚙️ working` with the profile's progress, and closes to `✅ (delegated to <profile>)` when the profile is done.

If the profile hasn't started on that request yet, its line says so in the meantime, as [When it is busy](#when-it-is-busy) describes.

#### On Google Chat and the A2A console

Each turn that becomes a task posts a status line, `⏳ submitted…`, which the gateway edits as the task moves: `⚙️ **working**`, followed by the agent's latest progress note. On Google Chat and on Microsoft Teams the line has a **Stop** button while the task runs ([the Stop button on Google Chat and Microsoft Teams](#follow-ups-while-a-task-runs)). The note shows only with `googleChat.mode: debug`, and that setting also governs the console when the gateway holds Google Chat. A gateway without Google Chat (Slack, or no chat backend) is rendered no mode, so its console always shows the note.

On the A2A console the answer posts as a message of its own, and the status line then reads `✅ **completed**`. On Google Chat the answer posts as a message of its own, with its 👍 and 👎 buttons, and the status line ends as a bare ✅. When the agent hands the work to a card, its one-line acknowledgement replaces the status line instead, and the card's plan and report follow. Nothing reads `completed`. Chat marks the edited line as edited.

A task that fails posts `❌ failed — <why>`, in plain words, or `❌ failed: <text>` when the reason arrived as plain text; one that is cancelled posts `🛑 canceled`, unless you stopped it (see `stop` below); one the executor refuses posts `🚫 the executor rejected the task — <why>`. When the agent needs something from you it posts `❓ <its question>`; your next message in the conversation is the answer. On Google Chat, the gateway rewrites the markdown Chat's text format has no syntax for. A heading becomes a bold line, a rule is dropped, and a bulleted list's marker becomes the one Chat draws a bullet from. A pipe table becomes a monospace block with its columns lined up.

On Google Chat, the agent's answer is one formatted text message. Its first sentence is bold, the rest is detail or an open "why" section with any table as a monospace block, and a next step or its options are buttons under it. The final answer to each ask, and each alert or report the agent posts on its own, ends with 👍 and 👎 buttons after any others ([Answer feedback](#answer-feedback)).

A card's progress draws one plan message edited in place. A card's report, the needs-you question, the pull-request message, the fleet-audit card, the first-run report and the stuck-task report lay out the same way as on Slack. The stuck-task report's [Retry and Drop](/kube-agents/concepts/chatops-slack/#stuck-task-report-buttons) are numbered. Google Chat draws no reactions on your message, since the app's credential cannot add one.

A first sentence longer than 150 characters is cut at a clause, as on Slack, or at a word when no clause fits. The bold line then ends in "…" and the detail opens on one, so no word of it is lost. The same cut holds on Slack.

A link button opens its link. A choice button is your reply: clicking it sends the button's answer as your message in the conversation, checked against the allowed-users list like a typed one. A click from someone the gateway would not answer changes nothing. The question then loses its choice buttons and shows `✓ <your name>: <the button>`, by your Chat display name (or "Someone" when Chat gives none), never an @-mention.

A reply you type in the conversation answers the question the same way, with your reply's first words, unless it is a command the gateway reads itself: `stop`, a status question, `/session`, `/feedback` or a `delegate …` request. A next step an answer offers takes no typed reply, and a question posted to the home channel answers only by its buttons. A question is answered once, by the first click or reply, until the agent asks again on the same message. A button on a message posted before a gateway restart answers the same way.

### Follow-ups while a task runs

A conversation runs one task at a time. A message sent while its task runs, other than the commands below, goes to that task as a follow-up, and the gateway answers `✏️ got it, I'll take that next`. The agent finishes the turn it is on, then takes each follow-up in the order it arrived as a further turn in the same session, so it answers with the earlier turns in context. Each turn's answer posts as soon as the next turn starts, and the last one closes the task. A task takes at most 16 follow-ups.

One the agent cannot take is answered `⚠️ not taken: <why>. Send it again after the answer.`, for one of these reasons:

- the task has already taken as many follow-ups as it runs;
- it was already finishing;
- the message had no text;
- the task's capability check did not pass;
- the agent's executor cannot continue a session (the bridge's `cli` executor; the default `api` executor can).

When the task ends, follow-ups that never ran are counted in `⚠️ N queued follow-up(s) did not run before the task ended; send them again if they still matter`, and follow-ups that arrived as it finished in `⚠️ N follow-up(s) arrived as the task finished and were not taken; send them again`.

If the task has produced nothing yet, a follow-up gets `✏️ steering sent — task <id> has shown nothing on its event stream yet, so no reply is promised: it may still be starting, or nothing may have taken it; if it is still silent 10m0s after it was submitted, your next message here starts a new task` instead. When that time is up and nobody has written since, the gateway posts `⚠️ task <id> has produced nothing on its event stream in 10m0s; unless it starts first, your next message here starts a new task instead of going to it`. The next message then posts `⚠️ task <id> has produced nothing on its event stream in 10m0s, so this conversation is released and this message is handled as a new turn` and starts a new task. The 10 minutes are the gateway's `A2A_FIRST_EVENT_GRACE`. A request handed to another agent whose line already says it is waiting (see [When it is busy](#when-it-is-busy)) gets that line changed instead of the separate message.

Besides [`/session`](#session), a bare [`/feedback`](#feedback) and [`/feedback summary`](#feedback-summary), two kinds of message are commands rather than follow-ups. Each must be the whole message; case and punctuation are ignored, and on Slack a mention of the bot is stripped first, so `@bot stop` counts.

- **`stop`, `cancel` or `abort`.** With a task running, the gateway answers `🛑 cancel sent — the task ends when the executor confirms`, and the status line ends at `🛑 **canceled**` when it does. The reply the agent is still writing stops too, and files no card after the stop; one that the agent had not yet started when the stop arrived, or an agent image older than this behaviour, can still file one. Your next message starts a new task.

  Nothing the stopped task writes after the stop reaches the conversation: no note, question or answer. Its line closes at 🛑 instead of a separate `🛑 canceled` message. A task with no line of its own, such as a delegated task on Slack, which shows task status itself, posts one `🛑 canceled` so the conversation sees it end. If the session had handed the work to other agents, one `stop` stops every one of those tasks too, with the one reply; the conversation can hand off new work once they have all ended.

  On Google Chat and Microsoft Teams the status line answers instead, as it does for the Stop button below: the gateway posts nothing, the line reads `🛑 cancel sent — the task ends when the executor confirms` with its button gone, and `🛑 **canceled**` when the executor confirms. A second `stop` changes the line to `🛑 cancel already sent — the task ends when the executor confirms`. A task with no status line keeps the posted replies below, as does a `stop` that the cards' stop answers (`A2A_CARD_STOP=true`, outside a conversation whose tasks go to a session worker).

  A second `stop` before the task ends gets `🛑 cancel already sent — the task ends when the executor confirms`. With nothing running in the conversation, `stop` gets `🤷 nothing is running`. In a conversation that has run a task, the reply adds that if the agent filed a card for it, that card keeps working until it finishes. A card outlives the turn that filed it, and by default `stop` cannot reach it.

  On an install whose operator sets `A2A_CARD_STOP=true`, `stop` stops the cards instead, except in a conversation whose tasks go to a session worker (the `/session` route below, or after `delegate`), which keeps the card notice. It posts `🛑 stopping this conversation's cards…`, which becomes `🛑 stopped 2 cards: <title>, <title>` naming up to five cards, with a note for anything it could not promise, such as cards that had already finished or a card that may still start. `CARD_STOP_ENABLED=true` in `install.env`, or the chart's `operator.cardStop.enabled`, sets it.

  With no card open it answers `🤷 nothing is running`, or `🤷 no card was open in this conversation` if you've asked something new in the meantime. Card titles are shown as plain text, so a title can't mention anyone or add a link; dots and colons in a title show as look-alikes, so a URL in it isn't clickable.

  With a task running, the cards are stopped right after the cancel. A second `stop` while the cards are being stopped gets `🛑 already stopping this conversation's cards — the answer comes when the executor confirms`. Once the first stop finishes the gateway checks once more for cards filed since, saying nothing unless it stops one.

  It skips that check, and says why, if you've asked something new by then, if the conversation has moved to a session worker, or if card stopping has been turned off. If the cards can't be stopped, or the answer takes longer than 75 seconds, you get `⚠️ couldn't confirm that this conversation's cards stopped; if the agent filed a card for it, it may still be working`. In the console the page may also show the agent's own short summary of the stop beside this reply.

  A `stop` acts only on the conversation it is sent in, so on Slack or Google Chat send it in the task's thread: a new top-level message in a channel or a DM is a new conversation. A top-level `stop` in a Slack or Google Chat DM starts nothing and gets `🤷 nothing is running here — each question runs in its own thread, so reply in that question's thread`.

- **Who can press Stop.** On Slack, Google Chat and Microsoft Teams, only someone who asked for work in the thread can press its Stop button: the person who asked, or anyone who sent a follow-up while the task ran. Anyone else's press changes nothing. The line of a task that has ended answers anyone, since it stops nothing. When the gateway can't tell who asked, anyone it answers in the thread can press it. That is where nothing has run yet, once any ask is older than a day (by default), or with more people or asks than it keeps track of. A typed `stop` is not the button; anyone the gateway answers can type it.

  What a refused press sees differs by backend. On Slack the presser gets no reply. On Google Chat, in a space or a group chat, the presser alone sees one line: `Only the person who asked can press Stop here. If it must stop, type stop.` Nobody else is notified. The button stays on the line until the task ends, so that line is what tells a refused press from a slow one. The line does not appear in a direct message, or when Google Chat names the presser by no user account. On Microsoft Teams, in a channel or a group chat, the running task's status line reads `✋ Only the person who asked can press Stop here. If it must stop, type stop.` until the task's next update replaces it. Everyone in the conversation can see it, and it names nobody.

- **Slack's Stop button.** On a Slack app that subscribes `agent_session_stopped` ([Slack app](/kube-agents/install/slack-app/)), the thread's Working… line has a Stop button.

  On an install with `A2A_CARD_STOP=true` the button cancels the running reply and stops the thread's cards as `stop` does. It posts none of the `🛑` messages and answers with one reply:
  - `Stopped.` when the work stopped;
  - `Stopped, except one task that didn't respond: <title>.` when one card would not stop;
  - `Nothing was running.` when there was nothing to stop;
  - `No tasks were running yet. I'm still answering your latest question.` when there was nothing to stop but a newer question of yours is running;
  - `I couldn't stop the work in this thread. Press Stop again.` when the stop could not be confirmed and another press can help, or `I couldn't stop the work in this thread.` when it can't.

  Pressing it again while a stop is still running adds nothing, unless you had asked something new since, which it stops too. Without `A2A_CARD_STOP`, and in a conversation whose tasks go to a session worker, the button does exactly what typing `stop` does.

- **The Stop button on Google Chat and Microsoft Teams.** The status line has a **Stop** button while the task runs. On Microsoft Teams the button is on the line's card, so an install with `adaptiveCards: false` has none; type `stop` there. A press sends the same cancel as `stop`, and the line itself answers, as for a typed `stop`: it reads `🛑 cancel sent — the task ends when the executor confirms` with the button gone, and `🛑 **canceled**` when the executor confirms. The press posts nothing else; a second press adds nothing.

  A press the cards' stop answers (`A2A_CARD_STOP=true`, outside a conversation whose tasks go to a session worker) is a typed `stop` instead: its messages, the cards stopped, and the button gone at the line's next edit. A press on the line of a task that has ended cancels nothing: the line reads `ℹ️ this task has ended` when the gateway no longer knows the task, or `🛑 **canceled**` again when the gateway stopped it.

- **A status question**, exactly one of `status`, `progress`, `any progress`, `any update`, `any updates`, `what is it doing`, `what's it doing`, `what are you doing`, `what's happening`, `what is happening`, `what's going on`, `what is going on`, `where are we`, `how's it going` or `how is it going`. With a task running, the gateway answers from the task's event stream without asking the agent: `🔎 task <id> is **working** (2m14s so far)`, the request it is working on, its state history, its latest progress note, and the line `(answered by stream replay — no live connection to the executor)`. A task with no events yet gets `📭 no events on the stream for this task yet`. With nothing running, a status question is an ordinary message and starts a task, except at the top level of a Slack or Google Chat DM, where it gets the same `nothing is running here` reply as `stop`. Any other wording, such as "how is the upgrade doing", is a follow-up, which the agent answers itself.

### When it is busy

The agent answers a fixed number of tasks at once: the Hermes bridge's worker count, 10 unless the operator's `A2A_BRIDGE_CONCURRENCY` sets another. When a new task starts while at least that many tasks from other conversations are outstanding, counting the running ones, its status line reads `⏳ **queued** — 12 requests are ahead of yours; I'll start on it as soon as there's room` in place of `⏳ submitted…`. It changes to `⚙️ **working**` when the task starts. On Slack, which keeps no status line, the notice posts as a message of its own and stays as written. The notice is informational: the task is already submitted. The operator's `A2A_BUSY_NOTICE_AT` moves the threshold. A conversation on the session route below never gets the notice, since it runs on a pod of its own.

A single request the agent hands to an AgentProfile, such as the `cluster` agent, has a status line of its own, on Slack too, and that agent may not start on it at once. Once the request has waited 30 seconds with nothing from that agent, the line reads `⏳ **waiting** — the cluster agent hasn't started on this yet` in place of `⏳ submitted…`. The gateway checks once a minute, so the notice shows within about a minute and a half. When other requests to that agent haven't started either, it adds `; 2 other requests sent through me are waiting for it too`. It says only that the request hasn't started, not why, and the number changes as they start. It changes to `⚙️ **working**` when that agent starts.

If nothing has started by `A2A_FIRST_EVENT_GRACE`, the same line becomes `⚠️ **didn't start** — the cluster agent didn't start on this in 10 minutes; ask again to start a new request`, instead of the separate message described above. A request whose line is read by a program, through the inject or A2A doors, gets neither, as with the queued notice. Nor does a request in a batch of several; for those, a status question lists each one not yet started as `waiting to start`.

### `/session`

By default every conversation's tasks go to the `platform` route above. `/session` moves the conversation to the session route, where the conversation gets a session pod of its own, started from the `chat` profile. The same pod takes each of the conversation's tasks in turn, so each one carries on from the last. The pod is deleted 30 minutes after your last message in the conversation (`spec.harness.tuning.sessionIdleMinutes`), and at most 4 hours after it started. Your next message after that starts a new pod, which reads the conversation so far.

A DM conversation with only one message so far is on a shorter clock: its pod goes 5 minutes after your last message (`dmSessionIdleMinutes`), since most DMs are a single question. Its second message puts it back on the 30.

An install runs at most 10 session pods at once (`spec.harness.tuning.maxSessions`). When a conversation needs a new pod at that cap, the pod that has been idle longest is deleted to make room, and its conversation is told `⏸️ this session was paused to make room for another conversation; your next message here starts it fresh with the conversation so far`. If every other conversation with a pod still has a task in progress, the new task waits in line instead, with one message that moves as the line does: `⏳ queued — every session worker is busy and you're next in line; I'll start if one frees up in the next few minutes` (or how many conversations are ahead). It starts when a slot frees, oldest first; `stop` takes it out of the line, and later messages in the conversation wait behind it.

After five minutes in line, wherever it is in the line, it is taken out with `🚦 not started: every session worker stayed busy while this waited …`, which also names any later messages dropped with it. When 20 conversations are already waiting, it is refused at once with `🚦 not started: 10 session workers are already running (cap 10). …`. Messages from a chat app, the console and the eval harness's inject door wait this way. A task sent through the A2A door is refused at the cap rather than queued, because an A2A caller is a program that reads that answer as nothing started. A follow-up to a task on the session route gets `✏️ steering sent — the worker picks it up at its next turn boundary if the task is still running`.

On Slack, start the message with the bot's mention, `@bot /session`, because Slack takes a bare leading `/` as one of its own slash commands. In a Google Chat space, mention the app as with any message.

| You send          | The gateway answers                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/session`        | `🧵 session route on — your next message opens a session pod; /session off returns to platform`. In a Slack channel thread it asks for a mention instead: `🧵 session route on — mention me in your next message to open a session pod (a Slack thread carries unmentioned replies only once a task has started); /session off returns to platform`. In a Slack or Google Chat DM, where `/session` sent top-level opens a thread of its own: `🧵 session route on — reply in this thread to open a session pod; /session off returns to platform`. |
| `/session <text>` | Turns the route on and runs `<text>` as its first task.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `/session off`    | `↩️ session route off — back to platform`. With a session task still running: `⚠️ a session task is still running — stop it first, then /session off`. When the route was not on: `ℹ️ not on the session route; nothing to turn off`. Sent top-level in a Slack or Google Chat DM: `ℹ️ /session off acts on the thread it's typed in — reply /session off in that question's thread`.                                                                                                                                                               |
| `/session` again  | `ℹ️ already on the session route`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `/session stop`   | `ℹ️ to leave the session route say /session off; to stop a running task say stop`. Nothing is stopped.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

If a task is already running on the `platform` route when you send `/session`, that task finishes there, and the message after it opens the first session pod; `/session <text>` sent then is not run, and the gateway says so. A message that starts with `delegate`, such as `delegate check the node pools`, sends that one task to a fresh session pod and leaves the conversation on the `platform` route.

### Answer feedback

On Slack, Google Chat and Microsoft Teams, the final answer to each ask ends with 👍 and 👎 buttons. That is the agent's own answer or, when it hands the work to a card, the card's report rather than its acknowledgement, once per ask however many cards it fanned out to. An ask whose cards were still running when the agent restarted gets none. The exception is a card that posts progress after the restart while the thread's latest question was also handed to cards: its report is rated under that question. In a conversation, plan rows, progress, questions waiting on you and notices have none. A tap from someone the gateway would not answer changes nothing.

On Slack and Google Chat, the agent's own home-channel posts carry the same buttons, labelled Useful and Not useful: an alert, kept through the triage that rewrites it, and a scheduled or audit report. They come after the post's own buttons, such as a drift alert's two, which stay first. Replies in an alert's thread, such as the triage card's progress, have none. A rating there counts as a rating of that alert or report, not of an answer, so a 👎 on an alert you found noisy does not read as a wrong answer. Its box says only the post is kept, since nobody asked a question, and its export names the alert's trigger rather than a question.

Each rating is counted. What follows is Slack and Google Chat; Teams differs, below. The button you tapped fills in. The gateway keeps your question and the answer with it, and nothing else from the thread, so an operator can turn the exchange into an evaluation case: a 👎 as a case to fix, a 👍 as a good answer to keep. A 👍 shows nothing more. A 👎 opens a box asking what was wrong (optional, one line), kept with it. The box says how long your question and the answer are kept, or only the answer when the question is no longer on record. Cancel keeps the 👎 without a note.

On Slack, Send thanks you with a line only you see. It also links the project's [feedback form](https://gke-labs.github.io/kube-agents/feedback) if you want to tell the kube-agents maintainers. Nothing is filled in for you, and the form opens a public issue, so leave your install's details out.

On Google Chat, the box is a card in the answer's thread that only you see. In a group chat or a space without threads, or when Chat will not take it in the thread, the card posts at the top level of the space, still only to you. In a direct message with the app, it is a card in the direct message itself, which only you read.

On Google Chat, Send or Cancel collapses the card to the same thanks line and the form link. If your note did not arrive with Send, the card collapses to "The note did not reach me; your rating stands as it was." If your 👎 was already replaced by a 👍, the card says the rating changed and keeps no note; if the write fails, the card stays for another Send.

In a Google Chat space, the filled button shows the latest tap by anyone, while each person's rating is kept separately.

Microsoft Teams draws the buttons while the gateway's Teams backend is armed. They are on the answer's card, so an install with `adaptiveCards: false` has none, and neither has an answer too large for a card. A 👍 is one tap. A 👎 unfolds a form inside the card, on your screen alone, with the same optional question, a line on how long your question and the answer are kept, and a Send button. That line always names the question, even when only the answer is on record. The 👎 is kept when you press Send, with a note or without one; there is no Cancel, and a 👎 you do not send keeps nothing.

After the first rating, the Teams card gains the line "Feedback recorded. Thank you." Everyone in the conversation sees it, and it names neither the rater nor the rating; no button fills in. A rating on an answer from before the gateway last restarted, or an old one it no longer remembers, is still counted, but the card does not change and the answer's text is not kept with it. A card that the added line would make too large is left as it is too. Alerts and reports on Teams carry no buttons.

You have one rating per answer, so a 👍 on an answer you gave a 👎 replaces it, note and all, and the reverse. The last seven days' count of answer ratings, such as "This week: 4 👍, 1 👎", is on the Slack Home tab only, refreshed every five minutes; Google Chat has no Home tab. On Slack, no scope beyond the ones [Slack](/kube-agents/concepts/chatops-slack/) lists is needed.

What a rating keeps can include cluster names and whatever else the question and answer held. It is stored in the `feedback` bucket of the install's NATS server, and only the gateway can read or write its records. The setup job can list the bucket's keys, which hold the task and a string derived from the rater's Slack member ID, Google Chat address, or Microsoft Entra object ID or Teams user ID. The bucket drops each record 30 days after it was given. Nothing is sent anywhere else. An install whose NATS server has no such bucket draws no buttons; the gateway checks for one again every minute for half an hour after it starts.

The counts are on the gateway's metrics and in the `PlatformAgent`'s `status.usage` as `negativeFeedbackTotal` and `positiveFeedbackTotal`; ratings on the alerts and reports the agent posts on its own count apart, as `autonomousNegativeFeedbackTotal` and `autonomousPositiveFeedbackTotal` ([Observability](/kube-agents/concepts/observability/)). To read what was kept, run the gateway's export in its pod. It writes a tar with a directory per rating, each holding a draft `task.yaml` for an evaluation case and the question, answer and any note as `transcript.md`:

```bash
kubectl exec -n kubeagents-system deploy/<name>-a2a-gateway -- \
    /gateway feedback export --since 7d > feedback.tar
```

For an alert or report, `transcript.md` holds the post and says nobody asked, and the draft's prompt names the trigger to plant instead of a question.

To turn it off, set `spec.integration.slack.answerFeedback: false` or `spec.integration.googleChat.answerFeedback: false` in the `PlatformAgent` CR, or the chart's `platformAgent.integration.<slack|googleChat>.answerFeedback`. On an install made with `install.sh`, put that value in a `TF_VAR_extra_helm_values` line in `install.env`, for example `TF_VAR_extra_helm_values='{platformAgent={integration={googleChat={answerFeedback=false}}}}'`. Answers, alerts and reports then carry no buttons and nothing is kept. With both platforms on, either field turns it off on both. Tapping a button already posted counts nothing, keeps nothing and opens no box; Slack still highlights the tapped button. Ratings kept before you turned it off stay until the bucket drops them.

Microsoft Teams has no field of its own. Either field above, on a platform the install has enabled, turns it off on Teams too; on an install with neither platform, set `A2A_TEAMS_ANSWER_FEEDBACK=false` on the operator, through the chart's `operator.extraEnv`. It is the same one switch: it turns feedback off on every platform the install has, not on Teams alone. On a Teams card already posted, the 👎 form still unfolds, and Send keeps nothing.

### `/feedback`

`/feedback` is the gateway's other command: how to report a problem with kube-agents itself (the harness, the agent, the operator, the docs) rather than with your cluster. Sent on its own, the gateway answers in place, starts no task, and whatever the conversation is doing carries on:

```
📝 To report a problem with kube-agents itself:
• Open an issue: https://github.com/gke-labs/kube-agents/issues
• Or use the feedback form, which needs no account: https://gke-labs.github.io/kube-agents/feedback
A report needs a one-line summary, the kind (Bug, Feature request, Question or Other) and what happened. A submission becomes a public issue, so keep cluster names, project ids and anything read from a Secret out of it.
To have it written up and filed for you, send `/feedback <what happened>` — I'll show you the exact text and ask before anything is filed.
For a usage summary of this install you can paste into a report, send `/feedback summary`.
```

Sent with text, `/feedback the cost report posted twice this morning`, the message is an ordinary turn and the Planning Agent answers it. It writes the report up as the form would receive it, a one-line summary, the kind and what happened in your words, says that a submission becomes a public issue on `gke-labs/kube-agents`, and asks for a yes. Nothing is filed until you give one. On `yes` it hands the exact text to the platform specialist, which files it through the public feedback form; the reply names the issue the form opened. The form needs no account and the install needs no GitHub credential for it. The same text sent again within a day is not filed twice: the reply names the issue already open.

The draft also offers to add the install's usage summary, with no names in it: the environment and usage sections of the block [`/feedback summary`](#feedback-summary) prints. The lines the agent cannot read from where it runs say `unavailable`. Say `add the usage summary`. The reply is an ack, and the block is posted into the thread when the specialist's card completes. Your next message, a yes included, gets the draft shown again with the block as its environment, and the question asked again. Only a yes to that draft files, so nothing is attached that you did not read. When the block could not be rendered, the draft comes back without it.

When the install cannot reach the form, the reply says that nothing was filed and gives the composed text back with the two links above, so you can file it yourself. An air-gapped install, or one whose credential broker has no route to the form's hosts, answers this way every time.

On Slack, start the message with the bot's mention, `@bot /feedback`, as for `/session`.

#### `/feedback summary`

`/feedback summary` is the one `/feedback <text>` the gateway answers itself. It posts one fenced block, short enough to read in full before pasting it into an email or a chat message, with no model in the loop. The block holds:

- the image tag, the mode, the Kubernetes version, and the install's age and readiness;
- the model as `provider/name` from the resource's `status.inference` (`unavailable` while the operator has not written it);
- the reasoning effort configured on that model, from the same status (`unset` when the config states none, `unavailable` with the model);
- the `status.usage` counters and readings;
- one `proposals by origin:` line, the origins whose count is above zero, as the agent reports them (`none` when all are zero, `no reading` when the status has none);
- the credential broker's top five `tool/subcommand` pairs since its pod started, from `status.usage.topTools` (`unavailable` while the status holds no list);
- the install's token and spend totals since install, from `status.usage`, which the operator keeps across LiteLLM restarts;
- a roll-up of the chat turns the gateway's own records still hold (ended, cancelled, active);
- a short feedback template.

The line is labelled `chat turns`, not `tasks`, because the script's `tasks` line counts kanban cards and the two pastes from one install would otherwise disagree under one label. The roll-up's window is fourteen days, or the install's session TTL when that is shorter, since the gateway deletes a conversation's records once it has been idle that long. The gateway's records hold a turn's start, a cancel and whether it is the conversation's running turn, not its outcome: a turn that ended any other way counts as ended, where the script's block tells done from failed.

It names no cluster, project, namespace, task or person. The gateway audits the block outside two lines: its `model:` line, which carries only the provider and model name from the status, and its `proposals by origin:` line, which carries only fixed words and digits. It refuses to post a block whose audited lines contain the install's own name, namespace, or any naming string from its declaration (the harness, scope, integration, security, telemetry and network-policy settings), and says so instead. The block's own words, such as a bucket or interface name, are not refused.

The one figure the gateway cannot see from where it runs, latency, is in the block `scripts/feedback-summary.sh` prints from a workstation. That block has the same environment, usage, performance and feedback sections, the same check, and more besides (the cluster scale counts, LiteLLM's live token, spend and latency figures and recent task rows). When the gateway cannot read the `PlatformAgent` resource, it says the summary is unavailable and points at the script. In the web console a `usage summary` button in the chat footer sends the same command, and the block it answers with has a `copy` button. The Hermes gateway (Slack beside Google Chat) does not read this command, as for the rest of `/feedback`.

### The Slack Home tab

Opening the app's **Home** tab in Slack shows a dashboard of the fleet, drawn without a model call, so it costs no tokens.

**Fleet** lists the clusters the agent manages, and any other cluster an open finding names, worst first. A member project's clusters are the ones the scope publisher listed for it in the resolved scope. The management project's clusters, and those of the projects `spec.scope.projects` names, which the publisher does not list, come from their Cluster Agents until those retire. A cluster whose name repeats across projects is shown as `project/cluster`.

Each cluster shows its worst open finding and how many more it has, or `no open findings`; past 15 it names how many more clusters there are. Until the first onboarding report is delivered, or its findings are in the queue, a cluster with no open finding shows `⚪ no findings registered yet` instead. In that time, until someone writes to the agent within a week of the scan, the panel adds "Send me a message to get your first report and findings."

A project whose clusters the tab has no list for is named instead of dropping out. Member projects the publisher could not read share one `(clusters unread)` line, which names up to three projects and then how many more; the management or an explicit project with no Cluster Agent yet gets a `(clusters not listed here)` line. Either line is drawn `⚪ health unknown`, never green, is always shown past the 15-cluster cap, and is not counted as a cluster: with only such lines, the count reads `No clusters listed`.

**Waiting on you** lists the questions with buttons that are still open in the threads where you asked, each with an **Open thread** button. **Running** lists the cards in progress, each with the cluster its agent is scoped to, when the tab lists that cluster. **Recent pull requests** lists the last five pull requests cards opened. **Last audit** names the fleet audit that finished most recently, with its counts and a button to its ledger issue. Any panel but Fleet is left out when it is empty, and times show in your own time zone. If the agent's pod cannot be read, the tab says so instead of showing an empty fleet.

The tab is drawn when you open it and drawn again, for about ten minutes after that, when a card moves or a question is asked or answered. Waiting on you comes from questions the gateway posted since it last restarted.

Only a member on the [allowed users](/kube-agents/concepts/chatops-slack/#allowed-users) list sees the dashboard; anyone else who opens the tab sees one line saying the fleet is shown only to the people the install lets ask it. With the list left empty, that is every member of the app's own workspace, checked with `users.info`; if that check fails, the tab is left blank rather than refused. A Slack Connect guest the list names as `T…:U…` sees it too, checked the same way. The app needs the Home tab turned on and the `app_home_opened` event in its manifest ([Slack app setup](/kube-agents/install/slack-app/)). Under the dashboard, the Home channel panel names where alerts and reports post and lets an allowed member pick another channel ([Home channel](/kube-agents/concepts/chatops-slack/#home-channel)).

With `KUBEAGENTS_DIAL=on` (off by default), the **Autonomy dial** panel sits above the Home channel panel. It shows what the agent may change on its own: Explain (finds and explains, changes nothing), Propose fixes (opens a pull request, your team merges) or Auto-merge proven fixes (your pipeline merges proven fixes).

Under the level, each kind of fix shows one repository's clean merges toward the auto-merge offer, for example `org/infra · 3 of 5 clean merges · off`, with a picker when the kind has fixes in more than one repository. The state is `off`, `Pending: rules PR #N` while the pull request that changes it is open, or `auto-merges`. A note under the row says why the last change did nothing, when it did nothing, or that the kind is stepping down (below).

When an auto-merged fix is still broken, the post-merge check opens a pull request that adds a [deny rule](/kube-agents/reference/automation-rules/#when-an-auto-merged-fix-is-still-broken). The row shows it as pending, with the note `Stepping down: PR #N was still broken after it merged.` Once the deny rule merges, the row reads `off` and the note `Stepped down: …`. If the pull request is closed instead, the row goes back to `auto-merges`. To allow the kind again, delete the step-down's deny rule from the rules file by hand.

A member listed in `KUBEAGENTS_DIAL_ADMINS` also gets the level's radio buttons and a **Request auto-merge** or **Stop** button for the repository shown. Everyone else sees the same lines read-only. The agent checks the presser on every change, and its one-line answer appears under the panel for the person who pressed. While a step-down's pull request is open, **Stop** answers that it is already stopping and opens nothing, and **Request auto-merge** is refused until that pull request is merged or closed.

### People seen

The gateway keeps a record of the people who write to it, so an install can count them. Each person who writes on Google Chat, Slack, Discord or, with the gateway's Teams backend armed, Microsoft Teams, once the gateway has verified them, has one hashed key and the time of their last turn, within an hour. A person is the identity the gateway attributes the turn to, so a principal map that folds two member IDs into one identity makes them one person. The key is derived for this record alone and matches no other pseudonym the install keeps.

The doors and the A2A console are not people and get no key. A platform the install still serves through Hermes, Microsoft Teams without that switch, is outside the record.

The record is the `people` bucket of the install's NATS server, and only the gateway can read or write its records. The setup job can list the bucket's keys, which are the hashes alone. Whoever holds the gateway's attribution salt and a directory of addresses can re-identify a Google Chat sender's key by hashing the directory, as they can the other pseudonyms the gateway stamps on each turn today. The bucket drops each key 30 days after the person's last turn. Nothing is sent anywhere else.

The gateway's metrics serve two counts, the people seen in the last 7 days and in the last 30 ([Observability](/kube-agents/concepts/observability-exports/#a2a-gateway-series)). Each is one number; no key is in a label. The keys derive from the gateway's salt, which the installer keeps across upgrades. If the salt changes, everyone gets a new key, and the counts read as new people for one window.

To remove one person, whoever holds the gateway's NATS credentials deletes that person's key from the bucket. The count drops at once. The delete leaves a marker that carries the key's name, and the bucket's 30-day limit removes it.

To turn the record off, set `spec.telemetry.peopleSeen: false` in the `PlatformAgent` CR, or the chart's `platformAgent.telemetry.peopleSeen`. On an install made with `install.sh`, put that value in a `TF_VAR_extra_helm_values` line in `install.env`, for example `TF_VAR_extra_helm_values='{platformAgent={telemetry={peopleSeen=false}}}'`. The gateway then writes no key and serves no sample. At its next start it deletes every key the bucket holds.
